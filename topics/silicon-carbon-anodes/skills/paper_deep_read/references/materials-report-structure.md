# Materials and simulation paper structure

Use these six source-traceable bilingual blocks for battery-material, electrochemical, mechanics, and multiphysics papers:

1. Paper overview: identity, configured-module alignment, conclusion, importance, problem, prior gap, contribution, and scope boundary.
2. Research system and problem definition: object, geometry and scale, phases and composition, process and loading, state variables, and target outputs.
3. Model and physical mechanisms: framework, assumptions, free energy, governing equations, constitutive relations, and coupling logic.
4. Computational setup and reproducibility: parameter table, initial and boundary conditions, numerical implementation, convergence information, and missing reproduction inputs.
5. Results, validation, and mechanisms: results, distinctly graded validation evidence, physical interpretation, sensitivity and uncovered questions, and experimental correspondence.
6. Research value and resources: value to every configured module, reusable elements, limitations and next steps, reproducibility verdict, key figures, PDF, BibTeX, and verified artifact availability.

Do not expose algorithm-paper labels such as algorithm steps, datasets, benchmarks, or theorem chains unless the paper actually contains those objects and the terminology helps a materials reader. Preserve equations and explain their physical roles. Module relevance is not overall paper quality, and an intrinsic continuum length alone is not a multiscale transfer chain.
